# Why I Started Researching LLM Interpretability

Large language models are remarkable — they write code, reason through problems, and generate text that reads as if written by a human expert. But when I started my Masters at Université de Montréal / MILA, the question I couldn't stop asking was simple:

**What's actually happening inside?**

## The Black Box Problem

When you ask GPT-4 why it gave a particular answer, it tells you a story. But that story is also generated by the model — there's no guarantee it reflects the actual computation. This is the core tension in AI interpretability: we want explanations, but the model is itself the explanation generator.

My research focuses on two threads:

1. **Mechanistic interpretability** — tracing which circuits and attention heads activate for specific capabilities
2. **Quantization effects** — how reducing precision (from FP32 to INT8/INT4) changes model behavior and whether interpretability tools remain valid post-quantization

## Why it Matters in Practice

At [Stingray Digital](https://www.stingray.com/), I build LLM-powered tools that engineers and producers rely on daily. When a RAG pipeline gives the wrong answer, I need to know *why* — not just that the embedding was wrong, but whether the model is confabulating, whether the retrieved context was misleading, or whether there's a systematic failure mode.

Interpretability research directly feeds back into building more reliable products.

## What I'm Working On

Right now I'm exploring how quantization affects the linear representation hypothesis — the idea that concepts are encoded as linear directions in the residual stream. My early experiments suggest that INT8 quantization largely preserves these directions, but INT4 introduces meaningful drift in some layers.

More on this as the research develops.
